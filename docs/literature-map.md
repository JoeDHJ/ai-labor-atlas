# Literature to product map

AI Labor Atlas uses labor-economics research to make occupation comparisons more useful without turning descriptive data into individual predictions.

| Research idea | Applied in AI Labor Atlas | User value | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Acemoglu and Autor, 2011](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Skills%2C%20Tasks%20and%20Technologies%20-%20Implications%20for.pdf) emphasizes tasks, skills, and technologies rather than treating occupations as indivisible jobs. | The explorer keeps occupation titles, representative task statements, structured skill domains, tools, AI exposure, wages, and projections as separate layers. | Users can ask which tasks and skills are shared before interpreting an occupation-level comparison. | The atlas does not infer worker displacement, productivity, or causal effects from exposure indicators. |
| [Deming and Kahn, 2018](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23328) and [Deming, 2017](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/132/4/1593/3861633?login=false) show why changing cognitive and social requirements matter for work. | The occupation bridge reports separate Essential Skills, Transferable Skills, Knowledge, Abilities, and Work Activities profiles instead of one text-similarity score. | Users can distinguish a broad reasoning or communication gap from a task-specific or tool-specific gap. | The structured distance is a descriptive profile comparison, not a measure of a worker's social skill or labor-market payoff. |
| [Gathmann and Schonberg, 2010](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/649786) and [Poletaev and Robinson, 2008](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/588180) motivate task-sensitive occupational mobility and human-capital specificity. | Career Bridge ranks occupations with weighted normalized O*NET profile distance, then shows shared task statements and software overlap separately. | Users can choose a focused work sample or training target and inspect why an adjacent occupation appears in the list. | Shared tasks are evidence for exploration, not proof that a worker can perform the target job. |
| [OECD occupation-distance research](https://doi.org/10.1787/d35017ee-en) motivates interpretable occupation-level similarity measures. | The bridge exposes the source profile, family-average fallback, structured similarity, software overlap, task evidence, and confidence label. | Researchers and job seekers can see data coverage and provenance instead of receiving an opaque recommendation. | A family-average profile is a coverage fallback, not an exact occupational equivalence. |
| [Deming and Noray, 2020](https://www.daviddeming.com/s/DemingNoray_STEM.pdf) motivates attention to skill updating and obsolescence in technical work. | The dashboard's training hint points users toward hands-on practice when task-specific or software gaps are larger. | A comparison becomes a practical learning prompt rather than a static similarity label. | The current release does not estimate skill half-lives, obsolescence rates, or individual training returns. |

## Design rule

The atlas uses the literature to choose transparent layers and cautious language. It reports what the source data can support, labels missing structured profiles and family averages, and keeps occupation pathways separate from personal ability, hiring probability, and employment forecasting.
